Start here.

Use the volume buttons to move, Play to choose and Back to return. Hold Back to go Home.

Home

Hold Back → Home

Home starts with Music, Mixtapes, Stats, Clock, USB mode and Settings, in that order. Now Playing appears above Music whenever a track is loaded, playing or paused.

  • Press Play to open the highlighted item. Music includes the Audiobooks and Podcasts shelves when available.
  • Hold Play on an artist or album artist for Play all or Shuffle albums. Both keep each album’s tracks together. Open Quick Settings with Menu on Now Playing, then choose Up Next. Settings → Home screen can also show the shelves and Up Next on Home.
  • The battery icon shows remaining charge. It turns red as charge runs low; a bolt inside it shows USB power. Choose extra readouts in Settings → Status bar.
  • Press Power to turn the screen off or on; hold it for two seconds to switch off.
  • For pocket listening, hold Play and Power together for about a second to lock or unlock the buttons.

A track is loaded

Home · while playing or paused

Now Playing appears above Music while a track is loaded, even when it is paused. Open it to return to your song. The row disappears when nothing is loaded.

Find something to play.

Browse your library visually or search for a song. Start from an artist, album, folder or playlist to keep that group together.

Cover Flow

Home → Music → Cover Flow

Flip through album covers. Albums without artwork get a colored cover with initials, using your current theme. The Random album tile picks an album for you.

  • Use the volume buttons to move between albums. Hold to accelerate, then jump by artist initial.
  • Hold to scroll faster, then jump between artist initials.
  • Press Play to open an album; hold Play to favorite it.

Search

Home → Music → Search

Find songs by title, artist, album, album artist, genre or file path.

  • Volume chooses a character; Play adds it and Menu erases.
  • Hold Play to run the search.
  • In long name-sorted lists, hold a volume button to accelerate and jump by letter. In the results, Menu opens sorting and filters. Hold Play on a song for Track info, My tags and queue actions.

An artist’s albums

Music → Artists or Album artists → choose an artist

Browse the covers in year order. The selected album shows its title and year. Volume moves, Play opens it and Back returns to the artist list. One-album artists open their album directly.

Hold Play for Play all or Shuffle albums. Both keep each album’s songs together.

Hide or delete a song

Music → hold Play on a song

Hide track removes a song from browsing and shuffle while leaving its file on the drive. Settings → Hidden tracks appears when something is hidden; choose an entry to show it again.

Delete file removes the audio file after a confirmation with Cancel selected. It also removes the song from the current queue; saved mixtapes skip it when opened. A chapter or CUE track sharing a file cannot be deleted on its own.

Up to sixteen songs can be hidden. After twelve hide/delete changes between scans, the player asks for a rescan before more changes.

Follow the library rebuild.

After counting tracks, Gnarlyr shows percentages for Sorting, Reading playlists when needed and Saving library. Once the tracks are ready, you can browse and play while covers wait for an idle moment.

Start listening while covers fill in.

The library opens as soon as its tracks are ready. Album artwork fills in while the player is idle and not playing, or after a minute paused. A key press, playback or a USB connection pauses that work; it continues later, including after a restart.

Embedded artwork is tried first, then a matching cover, folder, front or albumart JPEG or PNG. The player handles JPEG orientation and CMYK artwork. Until a cover is ready, the album shows its initials.

One album file, separate songs.

A CUE sheet is a small text file that marks where each song starts inside a single album recording. Put the .cue beside its matching FLAC, WAV or MP3 file, then run Settings → Rescan library.

In Music → Folders, open the CUE sheet to choose a song by title. Opening the audio file plays the whole album. Seeking, Up Next and resume use each song’s own position. A sheet must refer to one audio file; invalid track numbers or times and starts that repeat or go backwards are ignored.

Smart Lists

Music → Smart Lists

Five ready-made lists help you choose what to play. Each holds up to 100 songs.

Recently Added
New songs from later library scans, newest first. The first visit establishes the starting library.
Recently Played
Your latest songs, with each song listed once.
Most Played
Songs you heard at least halfway, ranked by play count.
Never Played
Songs you have not played, with newer additions first.
Old Favorites
Favorite tracks and albums you have not played in your last 500 plays.

Play starts a list at the selected song. Hold Play for queue actions. Lists use your last 4,096 plays.

Now Playing

Home → Now Playing

See the song, artist, album, audio format and progress. The top bar shows playback and connection status.

  • Play pauses or resumes. Hold Play to seek, choose a position with the volume buttons, then press Play to jump.
  • The volume buttons adjust loudness. Tap Menu for Quick Settings; hold Menu for Lyrics.
  • Change playback shortcuts in Settings → Controls.

Up Next

Now Playing → Menu → Up Next

This is the order the player will follow, including songs you add while listening.

  • Hold Play on an entry for its actions.
  • Move a song earlier, later or next, or remove it.
  • On the playing entry, Edit full queue turns an album or shuffled order into a mix you can rearrange.

Quick Settings

Now Playing → Menu

Change the controls you use most without leaving your music. Volume moves between rows; Play changes a value or opens a screen.

Change song or album shuffling and repeat Off, One or All.

Cycle EQ presets and saved curves, or choose Off or one of four headphone profiles.

Step through 0.5×–3×. Normal restores the original pace; recordings above 48 kHz keep normal speed.

Remaining Quick Settings rows

A different view of the same music.

Now Playing can show album artwork, a moving cassette, stereo needles or a spectrum. The time, progress and playback buttons keep their jobs in every style.

Choose your Now Playing style

Cover / Cassette
Cover puts album art beside the track. Cassette shows a wireframe tape whose reels turn during playback and fill as the recording advances.

Set the pace. Repeat the useful part.

Speed controls change how quickly a recording plays. A–B repeat keeps a chosen passage looping, and the sleep timer ends a session for you.

Playback speed

Settings → Playback speed · or Quick Settings

Choose 0.5×, 0.75×, Normal, 1.25×, 1.5×, 1.75×, 2×, 2.5× or 3×. Play in Quick Settings steps to the next speed. The choice is saved.

Pitch stays the same. Times, chapters and bookmarks refer to the original recording; audio above 48 kHz plays at normal speed. Read about the processing and player checks.

Faster playback choices

Sleep timer

Settings → Sleep timer · or Quick Settings

Choose 15–120 minutes, End of track or End of chapter/episode. On ordinary music, the chapter/episode choice ends at the current song; on a CUE album it ends at the current CUE track.

The last minute fades toward silence. End timers wait through a pause; timed timers keep counting. Off cancels the timer and restores normal level. Starting the player again leaves the timer off.

End-of-track and chapter sleep

A–B repeat

On Now Playing, hold Play to open seeking, then press Menu to mark A at the sound currently playing. Do this again later to mark B. The section repeats until you do it a third time to clear the loop.

Marks use the playing position, not a moved seek cursor. Changing tracks, restarting or switching to USB clears them. End-of-track/chapter sleep clears a loop so the recording can finish.

Save a mix. See what you listen to.

Save a play order, build a two-sided cassette or mark a favorite moment. Listening keeps your totals and most-played music together.

Mixtapes

Home → Mixtapes

Save queue captures Up Next as a named mix. New mixtape starts an empty one.

  • While naming, Volume chooses, Play adds and Menu erases; hold Play to save.
  • Open a saved mix to play it, edit it in Up Next, rename it or delete it.
  • Editing Up Next leaves the saved mix unchanged. In the mix’s actions, Replace queue saves your current play order over it.

Listening

Home → Stats

See total listening time, plays and skips, plus your top artists, albums and songs.

  • Open a top list to explore your most-played music. Moments saves exact places to return to later.
  • Export plays writes an Audioscrobbler-compatible .scrobbler.log at the top of the music drive. It shows progress, then the number of plays saved; each export replaces the previous file.
  • Back cancels an export and removes the partial file. If there are no plays to export, the player says so and leaves an existing file alone. Exporting keeps your listening history.

Two-sided tapes

Mixtapes → New two-sided tape

Build a cassette with a beginning, an end and two sides. C60 gives you 30 minutes per side; C90 gives you 45.

  1. Choose a length and name your tape. The usual naming controls apply.
  2. Hold Play on a song in Music, choose Add to tape, then choose the tape and Side A or Side B. Each side holds up to 64 songs within its time limit.
  3. Add a song to both sides, then open the tape and choose Resume tape, Play Side A or Play Side B.

At the end of Side A, Play flips to Side B. At the end of Side B, Play rewinds to Side A. Back leaves playback stopped. Resume remembers the side and position across restarts. Playing another list leaves tape mode; queue editing is unavailable while a tape is playing.

Saved Moments

Stats → Moments

Keep a shortcut to a favorite solo, a line in a book or any other place you want to hear again.

  • While listening, choose Save moment. The new entry shows the track title and time, newest first.
  • Select a saved moment to play from that exact spot.
  • Hold Play on an entry to delete it; Keep moment cancels. You can keep 64 moments.

Moments survive restarts and rescans. Keep a file’s path and chapter boundaries unchanged so the player can find it. A missing recording stays listed until it returns.

Clear listening history

Stats → hold Play → Clear history

Start the listening counters again while keeping your music and saved Moments.

  • Export plays first saves the current plays to .scrobbler.log, then clears the history after a successful export.
  • Clear resets totals, top lists and the play history used by Smart Lists.
  • Cancel keeps everything as it is. It is selected first; Back cancels too.

Moments, Favorites and an existing exported file stay intact. Most Played and Recently Played start fresh; Never Played and Old Favorites rebuild from the empty history. Recently Added still follows library updates.

Try a different sound.

The equalizer changes the balance of bass, mids and treble. Listen to a preset, then keep it or return to your saved sound.

Equalizer

Settings → Equalizer

The graph shows the chosen EQ curve. It describes the filter’s response, not a live measurement of the music.

  • Use the volume buttons to preview presets; Play keeps one and Back cancels.
  • Custom EQ lets you choose a band, press Play to adjust it and press Play again to keep the change.
  • Save a curve to a slot to reuse it later. EQ applies to music up to 96 kHz; USB audio uses its own path.

Presets and saved sounds

Headphone profiles

Settings → Headphones

Keep a separate setup for each pair of headphones. Choose Active profile, or open one of the four slots to edit it and choose Use profile.

Name
Choose In-ear, Over-ear, On-ear, Earbuds, Studio or Travel.
Trim
Reduce the level by up to 12 dB, in 0.5 dB steps.
Balance
Reduce one side in 1% steps to move the sound left or right.
Volume ceiling
Limit the highest volume step, from 0 to 60. Zero mutes the output.

Play saves a value; Back cancels. Profiles apply to wired music and USB audio, which keep separate volume levels. Bluetooth volume is independent of these profiles. Switching to a louder profile lowers those levels first; turn up again as needed.

Sound shaping

Settings → Equalizer → Sound shaping

Start with the change you want to hear. Each control runs from −5 to +5; zero leaves it neutral.

Warmth / Brightness
Make the sound fuller in the low mids, or darker and brighter in the treble.
Bass / Punch
Add deep bass or emphasize its impact.
Vocals / Air / Clarity
Bring voices closer, open the top end or reduce low-mid thickness.

Play starts an adjustment; the volume buttons change it. Play keeps it and Back cancels. Automatic headroom can lower overall loudness to leave room for boosts.

The rest of Sound shaping

Correction and saved sounds

Sound shaping → lower entries

Save to slot keeps the sound and slider positions in one of eight EQ slots. Reset shaping puts the sliders back to zero.

On top of adds your choices to a saved parametric correction, such as an imported headphone preset. The correction and active controls must fit ten filters in total. Reset keeps the correction underneath.

Parametric EQ

Settings → Parametric EQ

A parametric EQ lets you choose exactly where a sound adjustment happens and how wide it is.

Add filter / Type
Add up to ten filters. Peak changes a band; Low shelf and High shelf adjust everything below or above a chosen region.
Frequency / Gain / Q
Frequency chooses the pitch range, Gain cuts or boosts it, and Q controls the width. Higher Q is narrower. Disable a filter to compare without deleting it.
Preamp / Slots
Preamp changes the overall level; automatic protection leaves room for boosts. Save a graphic or parametric sound in any of eight slots.

Copy an AutoEQ ParametricEQ.txt or compatible Equalizer APO/Squiglink file into /EQ on the music drive. Choose it in Equalizer, review it and save to a slot. EQ applies to music up to 96 kHz; higher rates and USB audio (DAC) bypass it.

Remaining parametric filter fields

Gain and DAC filter

Settings → Gain · Settings → DAC filter

Gain changes the available output level. Low suits easier-to-drive headphones; High adds level and first reduces the volume. Turn up again as needed.

The five DAC filters offer fast or slow treble roll-off, low latency or phase compensation, plus Non-oversampling. The default is Fast, low latency. Slow filters soften the upper edge more gently; Non-oversampling softens the highest treble at 44.1/48 kHz.

Press Play to apply either choice. The output briefly mutes while it changes. The choice applies to both headphone jacks.

ReplayGain

Settings → ReplayGain

ReplayGain uses loudness tags to reduce jumps in volume between recordings. Off leaves their levels unchanged.

Track
Balance individual songs.
Album
Keep the intended differences within an album.
Auto
Use Album for consecutive album tracks, including album shuffle, and Track for song shuffle or isolated songs.

Without gain tags, the player keeps the recording’s normal level. Prevent clipping limits boosts using peak tags.

Make stereo gentler. Keep speech comfortable.

Crossfeed blends the left and right channels slightly for headphones. Speech controls shorten long pauses or even out voices in books and podcasts.

Crossfeed

Settings → Crossfeed

Off keeps the original stereo separation. Low, Medium and High blend increasing amounts of softened, slightly delayed sound into the opposite ear.

This is the default when no headphone profile is active. Each profile can keep its own choice. Mono plays unchanged; USB audio bypasses crossfeed.

Crossfeed in a headphone profile

Settings → Headphones → choose a profile

A profile keeps Name, Trim, Balance, Volume ceiling and Crossfeed together. Choose Use profile to make it active. Profiles created by earlier builds start with crossfeed off.

Spoken-word controls

Settings → Skip silence · Settings → Speech boost

Skip silence
Shorten pauses longer than a quarter-second to a quarter-second, with a short fade. Shorter pauses stay as recorded.
Speech boost
Raise quiet voices and reduce loud passages, with up to 12 dB of boost. Background noise below speech is not raised.

Both apply to audiobooks and podcasts, leaving music alone. Positions, chapters and bookmarks stay in the recording’s original time. Processing details and player checks →

One cable. Your choice.

Choose whether the cable only charges the player, connects its drives or carries your computer’s audio. On a computer connection, Ask highlights Connect to computer. Press Play to connect, or leave it for 15 seconds to choose Charge & play.

USB mode

Home → USB mode · or Settings → USB mode

  • Connect to computer: expose the drives for music, firmware updates and the companion app. Internal storage and microSD appear separately; the player charges while connected.
  • Charge & play: keep listening while the battery charges, without exposing the drives.
  • Run from USB power: use cable power with charging off. A low or unknown battery reading enables charging first. This choice appears with a cable connected.
  • USB audio (DAC): send computer audio to the headphone outputs. Select Gnarlyr as the computer’s sound output.

A normal selection lasts until you unplug. Hold Play on a choice to save it for future connections. Standard internal storage is named Gnarlyr; a card formatted on the player is named GnarlySD. Custom drive names stay unchanged.

To leave storage, eject every ECHO drive on the computer, or hold Back for a second on the player and wait for the chooser. For firmware updates, use the computer-eject procedure and leave the cable connected until installation finishes.

Storage overview

Connect to computer

Rate, graph and totals
See MB/s, a wireframe terrain of recent activity and the read/written amounts. Fixed-width digits keep the figures in place. Separate arrows light for reads and writes, and each drive’s activity light stays steady during a transfer.
Drive gauges
Internal storage and microSD have separate read/write activity lights. Free space is measured when the computer connects; it is not updated while the computer owns the drive.

Storage detail pages

The header’s speed icon identifies the link. Details show read/write totals and command counts, average command size, peak and average transfer rate, busy time, errors and USB link speed. The 480 Mbit/s or 12 Mbit/s link label is the connection mode, not a promised file-copy speed.

Holding Back asks the player to disconnect. It waits for three quiet seconds after writes, then shows Disconnecting before returning to the chooser. If it says Still writing, let it finish. Continuing to hold for three more seconds forces a disconnect and can lose recent changes. For updates, use the computer-eject procedure.

USB audio (DAC): use the ECHO for computer audio.

Choose USB audio (DAC), then select Gnarlyr as the computer’s sound output. High-speed USB offers 16/24/32-bit PCM at 44.1, 48, 88.2, 96, 176.4 or 192 kHz. Full-speed fallback offers 16/24-bit at 44.1/48 kHz. The computer chooses the format; the developer guide covers host validation.

DAC overview

USB audio (DAC)

The framed format shows what the computer negotiated. Stereo meters, player and host volume, the output jack and a buffer bar summarize the stream.

Tap Back for technical detail pages and again to return. Hold Back to leave DAC mode, or press Menu for sound and USB-mode choices.

DAC detail pages

Details include exact rate and bit depth, interface and channels, buffer average/low/high, clock correction, feedback, underruns/overruns, gain and session time. They help diagnose a stream; they do not measure sound quality.

On screen
Stereo level meters, audio format, player and computer volume, headphone output and active profile. Buffer, clock correction and underrun counts give more detail about the stream.
Menu
Adjust Volume, Gain, Output or Headphones without leaving DAC mode. You can also switch to Connect to computer or Charge & play.
Media keys
Outside the menu, Play sends play/pause to the computer. Hold Volume+ for next track or Volume− for previous; the computer’s app handles the request.
Volume buttons
Move through menu rows; Play changes a choice. On Volume, press Play and then use the volume buttons to adjust. Back or Menu closes the menu.

DAC mode remembers its own volume, starting at 35 of 60, separately from music. Start low. Headphone-profile trim, balance and ceiling apply; EQ and ReplayGain do not.

Bluetooth: press Play to reconnect.

If music last used Bluetooth, Play wakes the radio and tries your most recent headphones after its idle switch-off or startup. It waits up to ten seconds, then stays paused if they do not connect. Press Play to try again, or connect wired headphones.

A lost connection pauses music. When the headphones return, they reconnect without resuming until you press Play. If a connected speaker pauses its stream as it starts, the player retries that stream once.

Pause, rest and start again.

Screen sleep, standby and shutdown do different jobs. Choose what happens when you put the player down.

Power

Settings → Power

Screen timeout
Blanks the display while music can keep playing.
Standby when switched off
Off by default. When enabled, a short Power press or Auto off keeps the session in standby with the screen, audio output and Bluetooth off. Power wakes it; Play resumes.
Power off after
Fully shuts down standby after 30 minutes, two hours or eight hours. A long Power hold and the Sleep timer still request shutdown.

Settings → Power on is separate: it chooses Press or Hold when leaving the charge screen on a cable.

Start and stop animation

Settings → Power → Start and stop animation controls the wireframe opening and closing. It starts on by default. The opening runs for about 0.8 seconds; normal shutdown saves your session before the half-second closing. Charging starts, warning screens and shutdown from some menus use a plain fade. Low-battery and forced shutdowns skip the animation.

Charging progress

The charge screen shows a large battery, percentage and power state. After at least five minutes of consistent readings, it estimates charge gained per hour and time to full. Measuring… means it needs a steadier trend. This is an estimate from battery readings, not a measured charging current.

Shortcuts with the buttons locked

Hold Power first, then press Volume+ for next track or Volume− for previous. Release both before repeating. Hold Play + Power to lock or unlock.
