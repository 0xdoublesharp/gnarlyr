Start here.

Use the volume buttons to move, Play to choose and Back to return. Hold Back to go Home.

Home

Hold Back → Home

Home starts with Music, Mixtapes, Listening Stats, USB mode, Clock and Settings, in that order. Now Playing appears above Music whenever a track is loaded, playing or paused.

  • Press Play to open the highlighted item. Music includes the Audiobooks and Podcasts shelves when available.
  • Open Up Next with Menu on Now Playing. Settings → Home screen can also show the shelves and Up Next on Home.
  • The battery icon shows remaining charge. It turns red as charge runs low; a bolt inside it shows USB power.
  • Press Power to turn the screen off or on; hold it for two seconds to switch off.
  • For pocket listening, hold Play and Power together for about a second to lock or unlock the buttons.

Find something to play.

Browse your library visually or search for a song. Start from an artist, album, folder or playlist to keep that group together.

Cover Flow

Home → Music → Cover Flow

Flip through album covers. Albums without artwork get a colored cover with initials, using your current theme. The Random album tile picks an album for you.

  • Use the volume buttons to move between albums.
  • Hold to scroll faster, then jump between artist initials.
  • Press Play to open an album; hold Play to favorite it.

Search

Home → Music → Search

Find songs by title, artist, album, album artist, genre or file path.

  • Volume chooses a character; Play adds it and Menu erases.
  • Hold Play to run the search.
  • In the results, Menu opens sorting and filters. Hold Play on a song for Track info, My tags and queue actions.

One album file, separate songs.

A CUE sheet is a small text file that marks where each song starts inside a single album recording. Put the .cue beside its matching FLAC, WAV or MP3 file, then run Settings → Rescan library.

In Music → Folders, open the CUE sheet to choose a song by title. Opening the audio file plays the whole album. Seeking, Up Next and resume use each song’s own position. A sheet must refer to one audio file; invalid track numbers or times and starts that repeat or go backwards are ignored.

Smart Lists

Music → Smart Lists

Five ready-made lists help you choose what to play. Each holds up to 100 songs.

Recently Added
New songs from later library scans, newest first. The first visit establishes the starting library.
Recently Played
Your latest songs, with each song listed once.
Most Played
Songs you heard at least halfway, ranked by play count.
Never Played
Songs you have not played, with newer additions first.
Old Favorites
Favorite tracks and albums you have not played in your last 500 plays.

Play starts a list at the selected song. Hold Play for queue actions. Lists use your last 4,096 plays.

Play now. Plan what comes next.

Now Playing controls the current track. Up Next shows its place in the play order.

Now Playing

Home → Now Playing

See the song, artist, album, audio format and progress. The top bar shows playback and connection status.

  • Play pauses or resumes. Hold Play to seek, choose a position with the volume buttons, then press Play to jump.
  • The volume buttons adjust loudness. Tap Menu to open Up Next; hold Menu for Lyrics.
  • Change playback shortcuts in Settings → Controls.

Up Next

Now Playing → Menu

This is the order the player will follow, including songs you add while listening.

  • Hold Play on an entry for its actions.
  • Move a song earlier, later or next, or remove it.
  • On the playing entry, Edit full queue turns an album or shuffled order into a mix you can rearrange.

Save a mix. See what you listen to.

Save a play order, build a two-sided cassette or mark a favorite moment. Listening keeps your totals and most-played music together.

Mixtapes

Home → Mixtapes

Save queue captures Up Next as a named mix. New mixtape starts an empty one.

  • While naming, Volume chooses, Play adds and Menu erases; hold Play to save.
  • Open a saved mix to play it, edit it in Up Next, rename it or delete it.
  • Editing Up Next leaves the saved mix unchanged. In the mix’s actions, Replace queue saves your current play order over it.

Listening

Home → Listening Stats

See total listening time, plays and skips, plus your top artists, albums and songs.

  • Open a top list to explore your most-played music. Moments saves exact places to return to later.
  • Export plays writes an Audioscrobbler-compatible .scrobbler.log at the top of the music drive. It shows progress, then the number of plays saved; each export replaces the previous file.
  • Back cancels an export and removes the partial file. If there are no plays to export, the player says so and leaves an existing file alone. Exporting keeps your listening history.

Two-sided tapes

Mixtapes → New two-sided tape

Build a cassette with a beginning, an end and two sides. C60 gives you 30 minutes per side; C90 gives you 45.

  1. Choose a length and name your tape. The usual naming controls apply.
  2. Hold Play on a song in Music, choose Add to tape, then choose the tape and Side A or Side B. Each side holds up to 64 songs within its time limit.
  3. Add a song to both sides, then open the tape and choose Resume tape, Play Side A or Play Side B.

At the end of Side A, Play flips to Side B. At the end of Side B, Play rewinds to Side A. Back leaves playback stopped. Resume remembers the side and position across restarts. Playing another list leaves tape mode; queue editing is unavailable while a tape is playing.

Saved Moments

Listening Stats → Moments

Keep a shortcut to a favorite solo, a line in a book or any other place you want to hear again.

  • While listening, choose Save moment. The new entry shows the track title and time, newest first.
  • Select a saved moment to play from that exact spot.
  • Hold Play on an entry to delete it; Keep moment cancels. You can keep 64 moments.

Moments survive restarts and rescans. Keep a file’s path and chapter boundaries unchanged so the player can find it. A missing recording stays listed until it returns.

Books, chapters and lyrics.

Audiobooks keep a bookmark so you can carry on later. Lyrics follow the words in a matching timed text file.

Audiobook chapters

Music → Audiobooks → hold Play on a book

M4B books appear on the Audiobooks shelf after a library scan, including books outside a dedicated folder.

  • Select a book to resume its saved chapter and position.
  • Hold Play to browse chapter titles, then choose one to start there.
  • Keep the book’s filename and location to retain its bookmark.

Audio formats and preparing speech recordings →

Lyrics

Now Playing → hold Menu

Put a timed .lrc file beside the song with the same name before the extension, such as Song.flac and Song.lrc.

  • The text follows playback.
  • Use the volume buttons to browse lines.
  • Back returns to Now Playing.

Try a different sound.

The equalizer changes the balance of bass, mids and treble. Listen to a preset, then keep it or return to your saved sound.

Equalizer

Settings → Equalizer

The graph shows the chosen EQ curve. It describes the filter’s response, not a live measurement of the music.

  • Use the volume buttons to preview presets; Play keeps one and Back cancels.
  • Custom EQ lets you choose a band, press Play to adjust it and press Play again to keep the change.
  • Save a curve to a slot to reuse it later. EQ applies to music up to 96 kHz; USB DAC audio uses its own path.

All built-in EQ choices

Headphone profiles

Settings → Headphones

Keep a separate setup for each pair of headphones. Choose Active profile, or open one of the four slots to edit it and choose Use profile.

Name
Choose In-ear, Over-ear, On-ear, Earbuds, Studio or Travel.
Trim
Reduce the level by up to 12 dB, in 0.5 dB steps.
Balance
Reduce one side in 1% steps to move the sound left or right.
Volume ceiling
Limit the highest volume step, from 0 to 60. Zero mutes the output.

Play saves a value; Back cancels. Profiles apply to music and USB DAC, which keep separate volume levels. Switching to a louder profile lowers those levels first; turn up again as needed.

Loudness and library

Home → Settings

Adjust ten frequency bands and save a curve in a slot.

Track evens out tagged songs; Album keeps the loudness differences within an album. Off leaves the level unchanged.
Prevent clipping
Limit ReplayGain boosts that could distort the sound, using peak tags. Without peak tags, boosts stop at the original level.
Start screen

Listening comfort

Home → Settings

USB mode
Choose Charge only, File transfer or USB DAC while the cable stays connected.
Theme
Preview one of five built-in looks. Play keeps it; Back restores your saved theme.
Battery percent
Show the battery level as a number beside the icon. Low levels appear in red.
Rewind on resume
Replay a few seconds after a pause so you can catch the thread. Longer pauses rewind further, up to 20 seconds for music or 30 for books and podcasts. Turn it off to resume exactly where you paused.
Safe start volume
Choose a music-volume cap for startup and headphone connections: 12, 18, 24, 30, 36 or Off. Quieter levels stay unchanged. It does not apply to USB DAC volume.

Connections and customization

Home → Settings

Transitions
Choose Fade for a fade-out and fade-in between shuffled songs. Consecutive album and CUE tracks, books and podcasts keep normal transitions.
Transition length
Shown when Fade is on. Choose 0.5–6 seconds per fade; the default is 1.5 seconds. A fade takes no more than a third of a song.
When plugged in
Ask shows Charge only, File transfer and USB DAC when a computer connects. Charge only is selected after a 15-second countdown. You can instead always connect File transfer or always charge without connecting.
Headphones
Keep four profiles with a name, level reduction, balance and volume ceiling. See the headphone profile guide.
Format card
Quick-format a microSD card after two confirmations. Back up first: formatting erases its music, library, queue, bookmarks and mixtapes. Cards through 32 GB use FAT32; larger cards use exFAT. Pause card playback, finish USB transfers and charge above 30% or connect a charger. Keep the card inserted until it finishes.
Home screen
Move or hide Home items. Settings always remains available. Reset to default restores the original order.
Controls
Assign tap and hold shortcuts to Volume+, Volume−, Play and Menu. Audio shortcuts also work with the screen off.

Set the time. Keep your place in the day.

Set the clock on the player or sync it from a Mac. Home → Clock shows the time and date; Play opens a monthly calendar.

Date & time

Settings → Date & time

Enter your local date and time, then set the UTC offset: how far your time is ahead of or behind UTC. For example, Pacific standard time is −08:00; daylight time is −07:00.

Volume buttons
Change the selected value. Hold to change it repeatedly.
Menu
Move to the next field. Hold Menu to go to the previous one.
Play / Back
Play saves; Back cancels. A successful save sets seconds to zero.

The clock chip and saved offset restore local time after restarting. Change the offset when daylight saving time changes. If the clock says Not set, set it here or sync from a Mac.

In Calendar, the volume buttons move between months, Play returns to the current month and Back returns to Clock. Settings → Clock format chooses 12-hour or 24-hour time.

Make the screen and buttons yours.

Choose a look you can read comfortably, then assign shortcuts for the way you listen.

Themes

Settings → Theme

Choose Graphite, Neon Noir, Sunset, Sea Glass or Classic. Text size and Orientation control the layout.

  • Preview a theme with the volume buttons.
  • Play keeps it; Back restores your saved theme.
  • Language packs change interface text while keeping English available.

Controls

Settings → Controls

Assign tap and hold actions to Volume+, Volume−, Play and Menu. These shortcuts control playback; lists keep their usual navigation.

  • Choose a button action, then select the shortcut you want.
  • Use Default for the standard behavior.
  • Audio shortcuts also work with the screen off.

Home screen

Settings → Home screen

Put the items you use most near the top and hide the rest. Settings always stays on Home.

  • Play opens an item’s Show/Hide and Move actions.
  • Choose Move, or hold Play, then use the volume buttons to reorder it.
  • Play places it; Back cancels the move. Reset to default restores Music, Mixtapes, Listening Stats, USB mode, Clock and Settings, with Audiobooks, Podcasts and Up Next hidden.

Now Playing appears automatically at the top when a track is loaded and has no row in this editor. Audiobooks, Podcasts and Up Next can be shown here. Set the clock and explore Calendar →

One cable. Three modes.

Choose whether the cable only charges the player, connects its drives or carries your computer’s audio. On each computer connection, the default Ask prompt waits for your choice; after 15 seconds it stays in Charge only.

USB mode

Home → USB mode · or Settings → USB mode

  • Charge only: keep using the player without connecting it to the computer.
  • File transfer: copy music or an update. Internal storage and an inserted microSD card appear separately.
  • USB DAC: send computer audio to the headphone outputs. Select Gnarlyr as the computer’s sound output.

Eject every ECHO drive on the computer, or hold Back for a second on the player. Wait for the chooser, then select Charge only. From DAC mode, hold Back and release to return to the chooser, or choose another mode in its menu.

For firmware updates, wait for “Update installed” before unplugging →

File transfer: see when it is busy.

The cassette reels turn while the computer reads or writes. The screen shows Connected, Reading, Copying, Still writing or Disconnecting, plus the copied amount, drive sizes or Ejected, USB speed and battery.

Holding Back asks the player to disconnect. It waits until the computer has stopped writing for three seconds, shows Disconnecting for two seconds, then returns to the chooser. If it says Still writing, let it finish. Continuing to hold Back for three more seconds forces a disconnect and can lose recent changes.

A player-side disconnect may make macOS show “Disk Not Ejected Properly.” Ejecting on the computer avoids that notice. For firmware updates, use the computer-eject procedure and leave the cable connected until installation finishes.

USB DAC: use the ECHO for computer audio.

Choose USB DAC, then select Gnarlyr as the computer’s sound output. It accepts 16/24-bit audio at 44.1 or 48 kHz.

On screen
Stereo level meters, audio format, player and computer volume, headphone output and active profile. Buffer, clock correction and underrun counts give more detail about the stream.
Menu
Adjust Volume, Gain, Output or Headphones without leaving DAC mode. You can also switch to File transfer or Charge only.
Volume buttons
Move through menu rows; Play changes a choice. On Volume, press Play and then use the volume buttons to adjust. Back or Menu closes the menu.

DAC mode remembers its own volume, starting at 35 of 60, separately from music. Start low. Headphone-profile trim, balance and ceiling apply; EQ and ReplayGain do not.
