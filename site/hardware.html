Board components

What the datasheets, firmware map, device registers, and stock-code analysis tell us
ProcessorRockchip RKNanoD with two ARM Cortex-M3 cores: System Core 0 (rated up to 250 MHz) runs the player and audio processing; Calculation Core 1 (rated up to 500 MHz) runs the loaded compressed-audio decoder.
Firmware clock profilesGnarlyr uses 96 and 200 MHz PLL profiles. Light screen-off playback (FLAC, WAV, ALAC, and MP3 up to 48 kHz, without EQ or speed changes) runs at 96 MHz; Core 1 normally caps at 100 MHz with 200 MHz for heavier formats. Stock’s profiles reach 205.7 MHz on Core 0 and 480 MHz on Core 1. There is no dynamic voltage scaling; the SoC runs from a nominal 1.2 V logic rail.
Display memoryThe ST7796S-class controller holds 320 × 480 × 18-bit (~345.6 KiB) of on-chip GRAM and receives 16-bit RGB565 pixels over an 8-bit 8080 parallel bus. Gnarlyr updates changed regions without reserving a full-screen framebuffer in main RAM.
Shared audio hardwareThe 3.5 mm and 4.4 mm jacks share the two CS43198 DACs (one chip per channel), so gain and digital filter settings apply to both jacks. Gnarlyr’s Output setting chooses the volume scale for the jack you use.
Real-time clockA separate PCF8563-class clock on I2C1 (0xA2, 7-bit 0x51) keeps the date and time across shutdown from a 32.768 kHz crystal.
Charging controlThe board has separate on/off GPIO controls for USB power (GPIO2.0) and battery charging (GPIO0.14), plus a charge-complete signal (GPIO2.3), with no I2C fuel gauge or current sensor. ADC channel 0 reads the supply rail, so USB power can lift the reading above actual cell voltage.
USB packet memoryThe DesignWare USB 2.0 OTG controller reports 308 words (1,232 bytes) of shared FIFO RAM for receiving and sending packets. Its 480 Mbit/s link rate is separate from measured file-copy speed.

From music file to headphone signal.

The processor reads files and runs the menus. Two Cirrus Logic CS43198 DACs turn decoded samples into analog audio for an external headphone amplifier stage.

Processor platformRockchip RKNanoD with two ARM Cortex-M3 cores: Core 0 runs the player; Core 1 runs a decoder loaded on demand.
Audio chipsDual CS43198 in mono-per-channel configuration: Chip A at 7-bit I2C 0x30 (wire 0x60), Chip B at 0x33 (wire 0x66), on I2C0. The CS43198 supports up to 1 MHz I2C; stock writes at 300 kHz and reads at 400 kHz.
Chip identification · 4 GB unitID 43 19 80 · revision A1 · subrevision 06 on both chips.
CS43198 silicon ratings (DS1156F2)32-bit DAC rated at 130 dB dynamic range (A-wt), −115 dB THD+N, >110 dB interchannel isolation per chip, and ~26–27 mW typical active power per chip on 1.8 V rails. Two-mode adaptive Class H charge-pump rails (0x0B0000 = 0x1F enables HV_EN and external VCPFILT) scale internal supplies with signal level.
Why an external amp existsUnlike the CS43131 in the ECHO Mini (which integrates a low-impedance headphone amp), the CS43198 is a pure high-impedance driver rated into 600 Ω at up to 2.0 Vrms pseudo-differential. The ECHO adds an external headphone amplifier enabled by GPIO1.4 to drive 32 Ω loads up to 260 mW/ch balanced.
Audio transport & rate ceilingI2S1 carries 24-bit samples in 32-bit stereo slots via DMA, clocked from the SoC’s internal ACODEC PLL (the SoC’s built-in analog DAC/ADC goes unused). Although each CS43198 accepts up to 384 kHz / 32-bit PCM, the RKNanoD I2S interface caps out at 192 kHz, setting the ECHO’s hardware PCM ceiling.
Gain & output levelsGnarlyr uses the OUT_FS field in DAC register 0x080000 for Low (0x20, 1.41 Vrms), Medium (0x30, 1.73 Vrms), and High (0x31, 2.0 Vrms with +1dB_EN and HV_EN). The rated Low-to-High step is about 3.04 dB before downstream amplification. Stock writes reserved code 01 (0x10) for Low.
Volume, soft ramp, and auto-mute0.5 dB steps down to −127.5 dB (0x00–0xFE; 0xFF mute), with ~1 dB/ms hardware soft ramp in 1/8 dB increments (0x090003 = 0xEC), optional zero-crossing, and hardware auto-mute after 8,192 static samples.
Digital filters & EQFive on-chip interpolation filters deliver up to 110 dB stopband attenuation (fast/slow × linear/minimum phase plus NOS) with a switchable DC high-pass filter (0x090000 bit 1, kept on). Each chip also includes a programmable 7th-order PCM filter with three second-order stages and one first-order stage. Stock EQ runs on Core 0. Gnarlyr can program the three second-order stages for compatible curves on wired local PCM and USB PCM through 192 kHz. Other local PCM curves use software EQ. High-rate EQ and USB EQ still need player checks.
Channel balanceFiiO 2.0.0 uses the DACs’ volume registers to turn down either the left or right channel in 0.5 dB steps. Gnarlyr’s headphone profiles apply balance to decoded samples.
DSD playbackFiiO lists DSD64, DSD128, and DSD256 file support. The DFF and DSF paths traced in stock 2.0.0 convert DSD to PCM. The CS43198 has a non-decimating DSD processor with 0.5 dB soft-ramp volume and a 50 kHz SACD filter. It supports native DSD up to DSD256 and DoP up to 352.8 kHz (DSD128). The RKNanoD’s 192 kHz I2S limit caps the ECHO at DoP64 (176.4 kHz framing). Gnarlyr uses this path for eligible wired DSD64 files and USB DAC streams. Other supported local DSD plays as 88.2 kHz PCM. DSD64-to-PCM passed on an ECHO. Local and USB DoP64 have emulator coverage and still need player checks.

How the chip identification was read

Read-only registers 0x010000, 0x010001, and 0x010002 supplied the ID. Registers 0x010004 and 0x010005 supplied revision and subrevision. The driver’s wire-format addresses 0x60 and 0x66 correspond to 7-bit addresses 0x30 and 0x33.

Both chips on a 4 GB ECHO returned 431980 A1.06. A1.06 identifies their hardware revision.

Hardware controls in Gnarlyr

The ECHO keeps its processor, DACs, display, and battery. Gnarlyr changes how the firmware uses them, adding controls and refining the paths between music, headphones, and USB.

  • Guarded changes to the analog output. Stock changes gain while the headphone outputs are powered. Gnarlyr mutes and power-sequences gain changes, mutes around filter changes, and guards jack transitions while they settle. Low gain uses the CS43198’s documented setting in place of stock’s reserved value.
  • Separate listening levels. Wired and Bluetooth playback remember their own volume. Four headphone profiles add digital trim, balance, and ceilings for wired listening, including USB audio. The two headphone jacks share the DACs; choosing an output selects its volume scale.
  • USB choices on one cable. Move between storage, Charge & play, and USB audio. Run from USB power lets the cable supply the player with charging off; a low or unknown battery reading enables charging first. Stock already exposes the supply and charging controls in its DAC mode; Gnarlyr brings them into the player’s USB chooser.
  • A clock you can set. The clock chip keeps time while the player is off. Set the date, local time, and UTC offset, choose a 12- or 24-hour display, and open the monthly calendar from Home → Clock.
  • Finer backlight control. Ten brightness levels span 20–100% drive, with a ramp when the backlight turns on. Stock’s six settings top out at 95% drive. The display driver sends changed areas and adjusts its transfer timing when the processor clock changes.
  • Display transfers by DMA. Larger changed regions go straight from memory to the display controller while the main core sleeps. The panel sets the transfer pace; DMA frees processor time for the player. Small regions and transfers that time out use the CPU path.
  • Less activity between tasks. Stock’s main loop busy-polls. Gnarlyr lets idle cores sleep, batches screen-off storage reads, and adjusts the main core’s clock to the playback workload. The panel and backlight sleep between uses, the audio clocks switch off when playback stops, and an unconnected Bluetooth radio turns off after 30 seconds. Optional standby keeps a paused session ready on the 24 MHz crystal, with a later full shutdown.

The power and battery ratings above come from FiiO. We’ve measured processor load on the player. Battery runtime and analog output levels take separate measurements.

Tests on the player

On a 4 GB ECHO, Gnarlyr has passed 26 local playback cases and 16 combinations of sound and display settings with no reported audio underruns. The files included 192 kHz/24-bit FLAC, ALAC, Opus, APE, and DSD64 played as PCM.

On macOS, USB DAC playback passed all 12 tested combinations of 16-, 24-, and 32-bit input at 44.1, 48, 96, and 192 kHz. The same checks verified USB file integrity, the real-time clock, and rescanning an unchanged library.

During light 8 kHz mono WAV playback, the main processor stayed busy 38% of the time.

Audio decoding and output

Playing music is a relay: read a file, turn it into samples, keep those samples moving, then turn them into sound. The Rockchip RKNanoD splits that work between two Cortex-M3 cores, two DMA controllers, and dedicated audio math blocks.

  1. 01

    Read and decode.

    Core 0 (rated up to 250 MHz) runs the menus, storage, and audio processing. Core 1 (rated up to 500 MHz) runs the loaded compressed-audio decoder alongside the HIFIACC accelerator for FLAC and ALAC prediction.

  2. 02

    Keep a buffer ready.

    A ring in RAM holds decoded samples. DMAC1 moves them to I2S1 at the audio clock’s pace while the processors prepare more—or sleep.

  3. 03

    Make the signal.

    I2S1 feeds both CS43198 DACs (up to 192 kHz). They convert samples into analog audio for the external headphone amplifier.

Silicon subsystems and trade-offs

  • Dual DMA controllers. The RKNanoD includes two Synopsys AHB DMACs: DMAC1 (6 channels) serves Core 0 peripherals (Gnarlyr uses channel 5 in a circular linked list for I2S1 audio and channel 0 for display pushes), and DMAC2 (2 channels) feeds Core 1’s audio accelerators. Gnarlyr clears stale audio on restart, refills before restarting DMA, and fades around interrupted playback while keeping compatible gapless transitions continuous.
  • Hardware audio accelerators. Next to Core 1 sit HIFIACC (FLAC LPC prediction, ALAC adaptive prediction for orders 3–30 with lpc_quant < 12, and a 64–1,024-point FFT with ~14-bit twiddles) and MP3ACC (SYNTH polyphase subband synthesis and Imdct36). Gnarlyr uses the FLAC LPC block and enables ALAC’s predictor after known-answer self-tests. It intentionally leaves SYNTH unused (it drops MP3 output from libmad’s 24 bits to 16) and skips the FFT (its rounding exceeds our 2-LSB Vorbis tolerance).
  • Clock headroom and SRAM wait states. Rockchip rates the cores up to 250 / 500 MHz on a nominal 1.2 V rail (1.08–1.32 V). Stock’s clock solver pushes up to 205.7 / 480 MHz and enables multicycle SRAM responses at ≥180 MHz system or ≥300 MHz calculation clocks. Gnarlyr limits both cores to 200 MHz. Core 1’s governor can step down to 24–25 MHz when load is light.
  • Room for more features. On-chip SRAM is reused for code overlays: scanning, extra screens, and USB never need to reside in memory together. Scanning borrows Core 1’s 384 KiB bank while playback is stopped, and the display stores its own picture in on-panel GRAM.

Screen, backlight, buttons, and remotes.

The display holds its own 345.6 KiB picture memory, and a 10-bit SAR ADC reads the buttons, battery rail, and wired headset remote.

  • Controller & 8080 parallel bus. The driver speaks Sitronix ST7796S commands—the 24-command startup sequence uses ST7796S’s specific command-2 unlock bytes (0xF0 0xC3 / 0x96), enables IPS inversion (0x21), and selects 16-bit RGB565 (0x3A = 0x55). RKNanoD’s VOP drives it over an 8-bit 8080 MCU parallel bus on GPIO0.15–25 (8 data lines plus CS, DC, WR), with hardware reset on GPIO2.15.
  • Why the 49-pixel offset exists. ST7796S integrates 320 × 480 × 18-bit (~345.6 KiB) of on-chip GRAM. The ECHO’s 222 × 480 glass sits centered across the 320-pixel axis—(320 − 222) / 2 = 49 unused lines on each edge. Portrait clears the axis-swap bit in MADCTL and moves that 49-pixel offset to the columns.
  • Unused ST7796S capabilities. The controller natively supports 18-bit RGB666 (262K colors) and 24-bit RGB888, hardware Vertical Scrolling (0x33/0x37), Partial Display Mode (0x30), 8-color low-power Idle Mode (0x39), and Tearing Effect V-blank sync (0x35—enabled in the init table, though neither firmware reads a TE pin). Sleep-In (0x10) shuts down internal oscillators and DC/DC converters to microamp-class IC draw while retaining GRAM, and requires the datasheet’s 120 ms wake delay after Sleep-Out (0x11).
  • Backlight. A 25 kHz PWM signal on GPIO2.4 controls brightness. Gnarlyr gates the PWM clock off whenever the backlight is off.
  • Buttons & 10-bit SAR ADC. RKNanoD’s 8-channel, 10-bit SAR ADC (up to 100 KSPS, <900 µW at 3.3 V) handles three board inputs: channel 0 (supply rail), channel 1 (Volume, Play, Menu, and Back on a series resistor ladder where the lower key wins), and channel 2 (headset remote). Power has its own input on GPIO2.5.
  • 3.5 mm inline headset remotes. Channel 2 reads the 3.5 mm TRRS mic contact when a plug is inserted. Standard resistive CTIA/OMTP remotes (Play, Vol+, Vol−) work directly in both stock and Gnarlyr. On Apple EarPods, the middle Play button works, while the volume buttons do not because the ECHO lacks Apple’s ultrasonic handshake chip.

USB, power, and Bluetooth.

USB storage exposes the music drives to your computer. The USB mode chooser lets you return to listening or reconnect storage on the same cable. The firmware also provides a USB audio path; a separate module handles Bluetooth headphones.

  • USB controller. A Synopsys DesignWare USB 2.0 OTG controller with integrated PHY (480 Mbit/s High Speed, 12 Mbit/s Full Speed, 308 words / 1,232 bytes of FIFO RAM). Storage can negotiate high speed and retry at full speed if needed. While the DWC2 silicon supports USB Host/OTG, board-level VBUS boost wiring for host accessories has not been established.
  • Mode selection. Open USB mode from Home or Settings. Eject both drives before leaving storage, then choose Charge & play without unplugging. The current track, position, and queue are kept. Run from USB power uses the cable without charging; low or unknown battery readings turn charging back on first.
  • USB packets and audio DMA. The USB driver moves packets through the controller’s hardware queues. In DAC mode, the audio output still uses its DMA-fed ring; the computer has already decoded the music.
  • A different USB stack. Gnarlyr’s storage and audio code share a loaded module region. Storage activity, ejects, and pending writes are tracked before handing the drives back to the player. The cable can keep supplying power throughout.
  • Separate drives. Internal storage and an inserted microSD card appear as separate drives. Eject every drive present at connection to return storage to the player.
  • Audio mode. The stock 2.0.0 USB Audio Class 1.0 descriptor lists 16-bit stereo PCM at 48 kHz. Gnarlyr offers 16-, 24-, and 32-bit stereo PCM from 44.1 to 192 kHz over high-speed USB Audio Class 2.0. Its full-speed Class 1.0 fallback offers 16- or 24-bit PCM at 44.1 or 48 kHz. High-speed USB also accepts stereo DoP64 in a 24- or 32-bit stream at 176.4 kHz with neutral headphone-profile trim and balance. USB DoP64 still needs a player check. See the rates tested on the player →
  • Cable detection. GPIO2.0 controls taking power from USB and GPIO0.14 controls charging. Gnarlyr keeps these power-control signals in sync with the cable state.
  • Bluetooth module. A separate Bluetooth module (Bluetooth 5.1 in compliance report MTi260331027-0101E1) takes an analog audio feed from the DACs and receives short ASCII/binary control frames over UART1 at 115,200 baud. FiiO specifies SBC transmission to headphones; the traced stock protocol covers audio source pairing, playback, and AVRCP play/pause/skip—no BLE GATT or SPP data channel has been established.

Storage, 1 MiB SRAM, and real-time clock.

The RKNanoD has no external DRAM—everything runs inside 1 MiB of on-chip SRAM. Storage keeps music and firmware when the player is off, using two firmware slots so trial updates keep the previous version in slot 2 while the candidate runs from slot 1.

Internal storage & microSD busesTwo DesignWare MMC controllers: internal eMMC 4.51 wired 8-bit wide (two 5 MiB firmware slots, a SAVE area, and the FAT32 user volume) and an external SD 3.0 microSD slot wired 4-bit wide with active-low card detect on GPIO0.11. Gnarlyr reads FAT16, FAT32, and exFAT and combines both drives into one library.
Storage bus clocksStock 2.0.0 switches compatible eMMC to 52 MHz and microSD to 50 MHz High Speed. Gnarlyr uses a 24 MHz crystal clock for storage transfers. Bus clock rates and USB file-copy speeds are separate measurements.
Total on-chip SRAM (RKNanoD)1,024 KiB (1 MiB) total SRAM with 64 KiB/bank clock-gating, plus 16 KiB Boot ROM: 576 KiB for Core 0 (320 KiB IRAM + 256 KiB DRAM), 384 KiB for Core 1 (128 KiB IRAM + 256 KiB DRAM), and 64 KiB always-on PMU SRAM for low-power standby.
Core 0 firmware windowsData RAM: 0x03004000–0x0304C000 (288 KiB mapped; boot ROM uses its top during load). Main code: 0x03050000. Shared high/overlay RAM: 0x0307A000–0x0309EFFF (148 KiB) for screens, USB, glyphs, and language packs.
Core 1 decoder RAM0x01000000–0x0105FFFF · 384 KiB; also borrowed as scratch memory during library rescans while playback is stopped.
Real-time clock (PCF8563-class)I2C1 address 0xA2 (7-bit 0x51). Per NXP’s PCF8563 datasheet, timekeeping runs from a 32.768 kHz crystal down to 1.0 V at ~250 nA typical (3 V, 25 °C), with I2C up to 400 kHz at 1.8–5.5 V (stock uses 200 kHz). Includes a Voltage-Low (VL) clock-loss flag (decoded by Gnarlyr, ignored by stock) and century bit, plus unused hardware Alarm, Countdown Timer, and CLKOUT (wake-from-off would need an unconfirmed INT wire to power-hold).
Battery readingADC channel 0 measures the rail powering the player. On battery this reflects the cell (shutting down below 3450 mV); on USB power after charging finishes, the rail lifts above cell voltage (4.3–4.8 V seen). Gnarlyr therefore separates Charging, Charged, and USB power rather than showing rail voltage as cell charge.
What isn’t identified from silicon or public specs yet
  • External headphone amplifier IC: Enabled by GPIO1.4 and required by the CS43198’s 600 Ω driver rating, but the exact op-amp part number is not published by FiiO or readable over software buses.
  • Bluetooth radio module: Controlled over UART1 with a proprietary CS/CL/CQ/CY command set; exact chip/module datasheet and any non-audio capabilities (BLE/SPP/sink) are unidentified.
  • Charger / power-path IC: Controlled by discrete GPIOs (GPIO2.0, GPIO0.14, GPIO2.3) with no I2C fuel gauge or current sensor; exact charger part number is unconfirmed.
  • Internal eMMC flash part: Not yet cataloged per storage variant, though Gnarlyr’s eMMC driver already reads CID, CSD, and EXT_CSD during initialization—making future software-only flash identification straightforward.

Potential ports to other players

Close relative

Snowsky ECHO Mini

Why look here?
Related Rockchip firmware and existing community work make the Mini a useful starting point. FiiO lists two CS43131 DACs and a 170 × 320 colour screen.

The DAC driver, display layout, controls, and board wiring. Storage variants need separate checks of their firmware layout and recovery procedure.

Older players worth investigating

The xDuoo Nano D3 and IQQ C18 are additional leads from the hardware shortlist. What turns a candidate into a supported player?

First establish a reliable recovery route and a model-specific firmware image. Then bring up the screen, buttons, storage, and audio, and check playback, USB transfers, charging, and saved settings on the actual player. Shared chips help us choose where to look; those checks establish support.
